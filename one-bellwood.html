One front door: three doors onto one schedule

A clickable prototype of the access fix. Patients, outside doctors and Bellwood staff all read and write the same schedule in the EHR Bellwood already runs, so a slot taken by phone disappears online at once. Clinicians and slots are sample data Demo; this is not a booking system.

Why it works this way

  • Half of referral leakage is at booking. Of every 10,000 referrals, 2,600 are never booked within 60 days, out of 5,200 that never complete the loop Case.
  • Doctors say the same. 42% of referring doctors find booking difficult and 36% report gaps in follow-up, while 82% rate the care positively Case.
  • One schedule ends double booking. Phone and online read the same slots in the EHR. No second database to keep in sync.
  • Right specialist first. At least 40% of orthopedic patients put both expertise and rapid access in their top two (78% + 62% − 100%) Case. Options are ordered by subspecialty match, then earliest date, then distance.
  • AI suggests; people decide. The scheduler orders options and flags overdue referrals. Patients choose; clinicians triage.
  • Evidence. Centralised referral scheduling raised completion from 63.7% to 69.9% in a Midwest system Source. At Mayo Clinic Health System, 29.5% of online self-bookings happened outside office hours Source. In an Ontario eConsult service, 40% of eConsults avoided a planned referral, with a 2-day median reply Source.

Ortho capacity: lopsided, not full

The two orthopedic clinics together use 32,800 of 40,000 staffed slots, or 82% Case. Core runs at 94% with a 28-day median wait; Suburban at 70% with 11 days Case. Rotate surgeon sessions, steer willing patients to the earlier site, and move routine late follow-ups to Bellwood's own nurse practitioners and physician assistants at the surgeon's discretion.

Illustrative wait sensitivity

Checked and not used

Figures that appeared during AI-assisted research but failed verification against their original source.

  • "Orthopedic waits over 14 days make 22% of patients defect": the cited paper has no such figure.
  • "About $17M of downstream revenue needed per physician": not found in the cited data.
  • "Reminders cut no-shows by 29%" and "self-scheduling gave 4× bookings": vendor material only.
  • "Internal float pool at $140k vs $190k agency": no source.
  • "Denial write-offs 0.85–1.15% of revenue": HFMA publishes the metric definition, not this benchmark.
  • "$420–580 per excess hospital day" and "ASC rates 46% lower": could not be traced to the cited sources.
  • An inpatient +1 point margin gain stacked on labour and admin savings: removed as double counting, because the case allocates costs once across the four service lines.
